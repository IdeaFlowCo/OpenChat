A person's profile, as the place you keep track of them

A member’s profile has their identity and contact actions at the top, followed by what they are asking for that they chose to share with you, and a card that is private to you for notes, importance, catch-up cadence and links to people, companies, ideas and projects.

Built profile asks and private notes/links are in the currently served web bundle; PR #113 merged October 2. Proposed labels retain the October 2 design scope below.

What goes where

Their shared asks appear first (enhanced mode): text, remaining time and Respond. Your Private to you card expands into notes, importance, catch-up cadence and typed links to people, companies, projects and ideas.

Links have two meanings: a person’s self-shared website/contact links belong to their public calling card; links inside your private card are your relationship graph, such as “works at Northwind Solar” or “interested in Open professional graph.”

Still proposed: first-class standing asks you record for someone, such as Chet’s Sacramento ask, plus a dedicated “tools they recommended” collection. Those belong in your private section with source links, alongside their self-shared asks rather than presented as their own statements.

Why it stays out of the way

The profile is the clean, LinkedIn-style view. Everything you add lives behind one row that reads "Private to you". The summary line tells you whether there is anything inside without opening it.

Catch-up cadence

Fixed keeps the same gap. "Stretch the gap each time" is the spaced-repetition option: each catch-up lengthens the gap by 1.6, up to a year.

Links are your own words

"knows", "works at", "works on" and "interested in" are suggestions. Any phrase works. Typing a name offers the companies, ideas and projects you already saved, so the same idea is linked, not duplicated.

5. What an agent sees Built

Nine tools on OpenChat's agent bridge, using a key the owner issued. A key needs read to look and write to change. An agent acting for you sees your private card; it never sees anyone else's.
